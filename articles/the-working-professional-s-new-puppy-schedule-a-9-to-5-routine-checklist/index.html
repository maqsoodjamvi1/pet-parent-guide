When I brought home my first puppy while working a demanding 40-hour office job, I thought I could just wing it with a few extra coffee breaks. Within forty-eight hours, I realized that a standard workday completely conflicts with a twelve-week-old canine’s biological need to eliminate every two hours, chew on baseboards, and scream at the living room rug.

If you are staring down a hybrid or full-time office schedule while trying to raise a puppy alone, you need a battle plan. Relying on good intentions will lead to shredded furniture and chronic sleep deprivation. Here is the exact, hour-by-hour routine I developed to survive the workday while keeping my sanity and my puppy intact.

Morning Prep Before the 9-to-5 Shift

Your workday routine actually starts two hours before you log into your laptop or commute to the office. If you try to sleep until 8:15 AM and start work at 8:30 AM with a new puppy, you are setting yourself up for an accident-filled disaster.

I wake up at 6:00 AM for an immediate, high-priority trip outside. The moment my puppy wakes up, I carry him straight to the grass so he associates the outdoors with relief, rather than peeing on my hallway floor along the way.

Next comes a structured feeding and training session. Instead of just dumping kibble in a bowl, I use half of his breakfast for basic obedience training—sit, down, and name-recognition—which burns mental energy. Mental exhaustion tires a puppy out much faster than physical exercise ever will.

Managing the Core Workday Blocks

Once your morning routine is out of the way, you have to break the standard 9-to-5 workday into manageable chunks. You cannot leave an eight-week-old puppy alone for four hours straight; their bladders simply cannot hold it.

Here is what my daily checklist looks like from the start of the workday until the afternoon:

  • 8:30 AM - 9:00 AM: Final bathroom break right before you sit down at your desk, followed by placement in a secure playpen or crate with a safe chew toy.
  • 11:00 AM: Mid-morning potty break and a quick ten-minute game of indoor fetch or tug-of-war to burn off the first wave of morning energy.
  • 1:00 PM: The heavy lifting of the day—a full lunch break dedicated to a 30-minute walk, feeding the second half of daily meals, and heavy socialization or play.
  • 3:30 PM: Mid-afternoon bathroom trip and a puzzle toy stuffed with frozen yogurt or peanut butter to keep the puppy occupied while you finish your final stretch of work.

If you work completely away from home, you will need to hire a dog walker or enlist a neighbor to cover the 11:00 AM and 3:30 PM slots. If you work from home, keep your puppy in a designated puppy-proofed zone or a crate within your line of sight to prevent unsupervised wandering.

Dealing with the Witching Hour and Evening Wind-Down

If you survive the 9-to-5 grind, brace yourself for what experienced dog owners call the “witching hour.” Between 5:00 PM and 7:00 PM, puppies often experience a massive surge of pent-up energy, resulting in frantic sprinting, biting ankles, and general chaos.

Instead of trying to force my puppy to sit quietly while I answer late emails, I use this window for his most intensive exercise of the day. A long sniffari walk—where the dog is allowed to stop and sniff every tree, fire hydrant, and mailbox—tires out a puppy’s brain far better than a fast walk around the block.

After dinner at 6:30 PM, I strictly limit water intake about two hours before bedtime to help reduce overnight accidents. We transition into a low-key routine by dimming the lights, turning off the television, and initiating enforced quiet time in the crate.

Raising a puppy while working full-time is a marathon of consistency, scheduling, and patience. By breaking the day into predictable routines, you teach your puppy what to expect and protect your home from destruction. As you look ahead to implementing this routine in your own household, consider what tools, local support, or professional services you might need to set your daily schedule up for success.
